# Agritech AIBC preliminary video production guide

Prepared 18 November 2026. This is a recording and editing guide, not a rendered video.

## 1. The submission we are making

Produce one 180-second video for Competition 1, the Team-Built Product track. Use English narration and English captions, with a real Bangla voice demonstration and English subtitles. Use Agritech as the working product label until the team confirms its final name.

The supplied AIBC Participants Guide, Section 9.2, specifies this sequence:

| Time | Required segment | What our film must establish |
|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:30 | Problem | A rice farmer needs useful help through an interface they can use. |
| 00:30–01:00 | Solution | Telephone access, registration, confirmation, and focused rice intake. |
| 01:00–02:00 | Demo or flow | Actual prototype evidence, with clear labels for anything not yet demonstrated end to end. |
| 02:00–02:30 | AI approach | Voice, backend workflow, encrypted storage, and governed retrieval. |
| 02:30–03:00 | Impact and next step | A plausible payer, measurable pilot outcomes, and a credible expansion path. |

Sections 9.1 and 15 also require architecture, data governance, safeguards, and honest demonstrations. Section 11.1 allocates 20 points to each of five components; do not use the separate Single Prompt Challenge rubric in Section 10.9.

The guide lists the team preliminary deadline as 20 November 2026. It accepts an early prototype, mock flow, or walkthrough at this stage, provided the distinction is clear. It does not justify presenting a planned feature as a working product.

**Positioning:** Register the project under AgriSphere AI as a self-defined challenge for telephone-based rice intake and agricultural support. This is a cleaner fit than claiming the imagery-based Crop Guardian challenge or an already functioning climate-data product. Climate Smart Farming is a future extension once weather and reviewed climate guidance are integrated.

## 2. The story and the differentiators

The central story is: a farmer can make an ordinary phone call; the service first asks permission and learns who is speaking, confirms important details, and then builds a useful rice case for evidence-based support.

Open on the farmer's decision, not a dashboard or a list of model names. A yellow leaf is the scenario; the film must not declare its cause or imply that colour alone establishes a diagnosis. The emotional stake is the uncertainty of the next decision and the difficulty of accessing understandable information. Avoid invented crop-loss figures, staged poverty imagery, and claims that all or most farmers are illiterate.

### X factor 1 — Reach through the phone already in hand

**Message:** Designed for an ordinary voice call, without requiring the farmer to install an app or navigate the internet.

**Proof:** Film a physical button phone making the call and capture an audible exchange. A smartphone-only demonstration does not prove feature-phone compatibility.

**Qualification:** The backend and operator still need connectivity. Do not call the whole system offline, describe calls as free without a funded arrangement, or imply every carrier route has passed testing. The friend's failed-call issue remains unresolved.

### X factor 2 — Registration before personalization

**Message:** Consent, preferred name, approximate location, associated number, and optional age create the starting context for later support.

**Proof:** Show a case progressing from proposed information to read-back, explicit confirmation, and backend registration. Use fictional data in the film. Include a correction.

**Qualification:** A phone number is an association, not proof of identity. Previous profile access requires verification, particularly on shared phones. Database persistence has been tested; the complete phone-to-saved-profile journey must still be recorded successfully before it is presented as a live success.

### X factor 3 — Confirmation that the farmer can hear

**Message:** Important information is spoken back and corrected in conversation. The caller does not need to read the operator dashboard.

**Proof:** A clearly labelled scripted correction test and the corresponding pending/corrected backend values. Use the caller's actual words; do not fabricate a transcription mistake.

**Qualification:** Do not claim perfect dialect understanding. Familiar input names, local measures, and broader regional vocabulary remain validation and implementation work.

### X factor 4 — A rice case with explicit evidence requirements

**Message:** The system asks about the rice problem and checks the context needed for retrieval, rather than treating a fluent answer as sufficient evidence.

**Proof:** Show a supported symptom pathway, its missing-information question, and the backend response when no approved evidence is available.

**Qualification:** Current rice pathways are limited. Weather, regional soil/mineral context, fertilizer/product dictionaries, and comprehensive differential assessment are not integrated yet. The retrieval framework exists, but the reviewed agricultural corpus is not ready for deployment.

### X factor 5 — A backend that owns the records and retrieval rules

**Message:** The live model proposes facts; backend logic controls confirmation, registration, access, and eligibility for reviewed retrieval.

**Proof:** Proposed → read back → confirmed → registered. Show that incomplete registration blocks guidance retrieval. Include the encrypted-profile database test result, not a screen of raw personal records.

**Qualification:** These controls do not guarantee approval of every sentence Gemini speaks. Never say the product is hallucination-free or that every spoken answer is expert-approved.

### X factor 6 — A service channel institutions could support

**Message:** A cooperative, NGO, extension programme, or telephone service partner could sponsor access and operate the support workflow.

**Proof:** A simple proposed payer diagram and a pilot measurement plan.

**Qualification:** These are prospective customers and partners, not signed partnerships. No validated savings, yield increase, revenue, retention, or unit economics have been established. Advice must remain independent of product sales incentives.

## 3. The visual direction

Use documentary-style footage, clean screen captures, and restrained diagrams. A calm, confident narrator should sound like a builder explaining a real system. Avoid a dramatic AI-generated farmer testimonial or fabricated field footage presented as user research.

Use a rice green, warm neutral background, and charcoal text. Reserve amber for a clearly labelled planned capability or prototype limitation. Keep on-screen headlines short. Do not display the rubric as a slide in the main film; demonstrate its criteria through the story.

Record at 1920 × 1080, 16:9, 25 or 30 fps. These are production defaults, not extra rules attributed to the guide. Keep essential text away from the frame edges. For a 1080p frame, use approximately 44–60 px headlines and 36–44 px captions; inspect them at normal playback size. Use a Bengali font such as Noto Sans Bengali for Bangla, checking the installed font before editing.

English narration carries the judging argument. Bangla belongs in the actual farmer interaction. Subtitle all Bangla speech accurately in English. Retain **আসসালামু আলাইকুম** as the opening greeting. Keep music quiet and remove it during the most important phone exchange. Do not speed up spoken farmer audio to force it into the time limit.

### The demo has two permitted cuts

**Cut A — complete registration demonstration:** Use only after an authenticated case-bound phone call has successfully collected, read back, confirmed, registered, and reloaded a synthetic profile. Show the real corresponding operator state. This is the strongest version.

**Cut B — evidence-backed current-state demonstration:** If that journey has not passed, combine a genuine phone-voice clip, the actual encrypted-profile persistence test result, and a labelled diagram of the intended registration flow. Say explicitly that phone-to-registration validation is the next milestone. The preliminary guide permits this. Do not edit separate clips to imply a single successful end-to-end run.

The standalone script includes wording for both cuts. Select one version before recording narration.

## 7. Impact and business claims

Pitch the first service as a supervised rice intake and support workflow. A prospective cooperative, NGO, extension organisation, or telephone partner would pay for access and operation. The farmer uses an ordinary call; whether their call is subsidised is a contract and pricing decision, not an existing free service.

Use one business sentence in the film. Keep the supporting calculations for judge questions:

**Cost per completed confirmed case = telephone cost + model/audio cost + hosting + allocated specialist/operator time, divided by completed confirmed cases.**

Do not calculate that cost from token prices alone. Do not monetise private farmer information or imply that selling inputs is necessary for support.

### Proposed validation plan

A small initial study could recruit 20 consenting farmers across two contrasting settings, with a qualified agricultural specialist and the necessary ethics/research permissions. This is a proposed study, not a committed partnership, representative national sample, or completed pilot. Keep a normal-service comparison where feasible and report limitations.

| Measure | Definition | How to present it now |
|---|---|---|
| Registration completion | Confirmed saved profiles / consented attempted registrations, including failures. | Planned pilot KPI. |
| Critical-fact accuracy | Correctly captured and confirmed fields / independently checked critical fields. | Planned pilot KPI; include numbers, negation and corrections. |
| Reply delay | End of caller speech to first audible AI reply at the handset; report median and p95. | Planned phone metric. Text-to-first-audio tests are not a substitute. |
| Comprehension | Caller can explain the agreed next step in their own words. | Planned teach-back measure. |
| Escalation quality | Specialist assessment of appropriate uncertainty/referral handling. | Planned reviewed measure. A request is not a completed referral. |
| Cost per completed case | Full operating cost divided by completed confirmed cases. | Planned commercial metric. |
| Agronomic outcome | A separately designed study of input use, losses or yield across an appropriate period. | Future validation; no yield/savings claim in this preliminary film. |

The currently verified software evidence is 47 passing app tests, applied database migrations, access-control checks, and a synthetic encrypted-profile persistence check. These establish software behaviour, not farmer benefit, dialect accuracy, or production safety.

## 8. Architecture and roadmap without overclaiming

Use a diagram with only five blocks:

**Farmer's phone → Alaap telephone bridge → Gemini Live voice → backend workflow → encrypted case records and governed retrieval.**

Draw operator review beside the backend. On the farmer side, show spoken questions and spoken read-back. The web console is an operator tool; it is not something the farmer must navigate.

Label the current system as operator-supervised and single-call. State in supporting documentation that Google processes audio in configured cloud regions. Encrypting stored profiles is not the same as local-only processing, legal compliance certification, or full data sovereignty.

Show weather, region/soil context, reviewed input-name dictionaries, and fuller rice reasoning in a separate “Next” panel. Fertilizer history is not a soil nutrient measurement. A local nickname or packet colour does not establish a product's identity, concentration or dose.

BanglaBERT, BanglaT5, and BanglaLlama belong in the research appendix, not the main claim of the film. Their checkpoints have run locally, but task-specific training and dialect/agricultural accuracy evaluation remain incomplete. They are not the current voice engine. Do not describe development tools or MCP access used by builders as a deployed multi-agent runtime.

## 9. Capture and edit instructions

1. Use fictional registration details. Obtain any actor's permission for footage and voice. Label acted material and synthetic records.
2. Sign in to the operator console. Create an explicitly synthetic case with storage consent and open its case-bound telephone link. Ordinary voice-only mode does not prove profiling.
3. Record the whole registration attempt before selecting excerpts. Check the saved state, correction history and case association. Reopen the case to verify persistence.
4. Record the phone and direct audio together where possible. Keep an unedited reference take. Use headphones to avoid speaker-to-phone echo.
5. Record the rice clarification and the no-approved-evidence boundary. Do not populate a fake “approved” passage for filming.
6. Capture a clean architecture diagram, source review status, and actual test results. Avoid scrolling through source code during the main demo.
7. Hide access tokens, passwords, service-account paths, email sign-in links, raw call identifiers and real phone numbers. Crop them out before recording wherever possible; inspect exported frames for missed disclosures.
8. Build the edit against the five fixed time windows. Use the phone exchange as the main proof, with narration paused underneath it. If gaps are shortened, label that; do not conceal a functional failure by stitching together unrelated successful moments.
9. Record the narrator after the edit timing is stable. Rehearse at a natural pace. The separate script reserves time for original Bangla audio and pauses; do not fill every second with English narration.
10. Export a 1080p H.264 MP4 with AAC audio. Watch the actual exported file from beginning to end on a laptop and a phone. Confirm duration is at most 180 seconds, captions are legible, and speech is intelligible through ordinary speakers.

## 10. Recording gate and submission check

**Before using Cut A:** phone routing works on the demonstrated route; a physical phone connects; the selected registration case is authenticated; consent, details, read-back, correction, confirmation and save all complete; the reopened case shows the saved profile; no unsupported treatment is spoken.

**If any gate fails:** use Cut B with clear prototype/test/flow labels. Retain the same 180-second structure. The competition rewards honest execution evidence; an unfinished but accurately shown system is preferable to a fabricated success.

Before upload, confirm:

- All five official segments are present and the file is no longer than 180 seconds.
- The core use case is rice support through an ordinary call, with profiling before guidance.
- Every measured number is traceable to an actual result; all targets and future capabilities are labelled.
- Neither the voice demo nor the architecture implies complete offline operation, trained dialect models, reviewed dosing, real-time weather, sensor deployment, or national rollout.
- The film contains no secrets, real private profiles, unlicensed footage or unverified partner claims.
- The one-page summary matches the film and includes the team’s named ownership of all five required roles: leader/coordinator; business/data; UI/UX/frontend; backend/data; presentation/communication. A member may hold multiple roles.
- Any repository or demo link is usable by judges without exposing private data. The current localhost operator console is not a public judging link.

## 11. Prepare these judge answers

**What is actually working?** Telephone voice integration, Bangla audio/transcripts, backend confirmation and registration rules, encrypted profile storage, source/review and retrieval infrastructure. Show the exact demonstration evidence and distinguish the end-to-end phone validation still needed.

**Why use AI?** Conversational Bangla speech, flexible intake and clarification, structured fact proposals, and retrieval over contextual knowledge require more than a fixed menu. Backend controls retain responsibility for data state and evidence eligibility.

**What happens if the model is wrong?** Critical facts are read back, corrections are retained, and uncertainty can lead to a specialist boundary. Spoken output is still model-controlled; field validation and stronger safeguards remain necessary.

**What happens if cloud capacity fails?** Startup retries are bounded. Existing conversations are not silently replayed. The separate free Gemini Developer route is limited to explicitly synthetic cases because its data-use terms differ. Do not promise uninterrupted service.

**How do you know the caller has a button phone?** The present gateway metadata does not establish handset type. Ask an optional capability question or use a carrier-supported interface only if a suitable integration becomes available. Never infer literacy or ownership from a number.

**Why will a farmer trust this?** Trust is a validation goal supported by understandable speech, consent, correction, source provenance and specialist involvement. It is not established by an AI voice sounding confident.

**How will this scale?** Replace the local browser bridge with a measured, supervised service deployment; validate concurrency, monitoring and costs; recruit content reviewers and service partners; adapt language, crops, local units and governance for each market. That is the roadmap, not the current deployment.
