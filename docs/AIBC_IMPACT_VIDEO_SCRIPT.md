# Agritech impact video master script

## Purpose and recording decision

This is the revised master narration for the AIBC Team Competition preliminary video. It replaces the earlier narration in `AIBC_180_SECOND_SCRIPT.md`; keep the production details in `AIBC_VIDEO_GUIDE.md` alongside it. Target length is 180 seconds, following the official five windows. Narration is English; the product demonstration is Bangla with accurate English subtitles.

**Creative idea:** Start with the person who grows our food. During the pitch, a teammate makes an ordinary phone call and lets the product be heard. Finish by returning to that same farmer. The product experience supplies the evidence for the pitch, inspired by Jim and Dwight's demonstration-led sales scene, without recreating their dialogue or staging a competitor's poor service.

**Main argument:** The knowledge gap includes access, reading, language, interpretation and trust. A farmer's practical agricultural knowledge deserves respect. Voice access, confirmed context and a governed support workflow offer a concrete way to address those barriers. The prototype is not a demonstrated cure for poverty or crop loss.

**Current-evidence edit:** The script below uses the verified prototype and backend tests without claiming that a complete phone-to-saved-profile recording already exists. A short upgrade line is provided for use only after that recording is verified. Real phone audio must be captured, not generated and presented as a real call. Label scripted demonstrations and shortened pauses.

## The opening and closing callback

Opening thought: **The farmer who feeds you should not have to gamble his family's next meal on a guess.**

Closing thought: **He already knows how to feed us. We are building a way for the world's knowledge to reach him.**

Use the same farmer and phone in both shots. The emotional movement is from exclusion to an accessible channel, rather than from helplessness to rescue. If the actor is a woman, change the pronouns consistently; do not change the underlying argument.

### Delivery

The first sentence is direct and quiet. Pause after “a guess.” Do not make the farmer sound unintelligent. The financial line is a conditional consequence of limited resources, not a claim that all farmers are hungry or that all crop problems are caused by poor knowledge.

## 00:30–01:00 Solution and the call begins

### Picture and action

Cut to two team members in a simple presentation setting. Presenter A speaks to camera. Presenter B holds a button phone and prepares to call. Keep the shot natural; avoid costumes or an imitation of The Office characters.

Use four concise visual stages as the narration proceeds:

**Call → Consent → Confirmed profile → Rice case**

A small second panel identifies the web interface as the operator console. The farmer is not expected to use it. Near the end of this segment, Presenter B dials the prototype. An operator prepares the AI session. Include “Operator-supervised prototype” as a small status label; the current system is not an unattended call-centre deployment.

### Spoken narration

> Agritech brings rice-support intake to an ordinary phone call, in Bangla.
>
> It asks permission, builds a profile, and reads important details back. The farmer can correct it.
>
> Someone who struggles with text can still ask a question, describe a symptom, and be heard.
>
> My teammate is calling our supervised prototype now.

### Delivery

The tone becomes practical and assured. On “calling,” actually begin the call. Do not promise an instant answer or compare response speed with another service. Let the observed connection time determine the edit. If pauses are shortened, label that fact.

## Supporting evidence and statistical guardrails

### Scale of farming

Bangladesh Agricultural Census 2019 main results list **16,881,757 farm holdings**. Use the rounded “almost seventeen million farm holdings” in narration and the date on screen. Holdings are farming units, not a count of individual farmers. They are not all rice farmers, illiterate people, poor households, or customers the prototype has reached. Do not add seasonal rice-holding counts together because holdings can appear in more than one season.

Source: [FAO publication of Bangladesh Agricultural Census 2019 main results](https://www.fao.org/fileadmin/templates/ess/ess_test_folder/World_Census_Agriculture/WCA_2020/WCA2020_TMRs/TMR_Bangladesh_2019_12052023.pdf).

### Economic vulnerability

The World Bank's November 2025 assessment announcement reports nearly **62 million Bangladeshis** vulnerable to falling back into poverty after shocks. This is national context, not a count of vulnerable farmers or a synonym for food insecurity. Use it in supporting slides, the one-page summary or judge questions; do not overcrowd the opening with another large number.

Suggested supporting-slide wording: **National context: nearly 62 million people vulnerable to falling back into poverty — World Bank, 2025.**

Source: [World Bank Bangladesh Poverty and Equity Assessment announcement](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2025/11/25/job-creation-key-to-equality-and-faster-poverty-reduction-in-bangladesh-says-world-bank).

### Literacy and practical digital access

GSMA identifies literacy and digital skills as barriers to mobile-internet adoption and use in Bangladesh. Its Mobile Gender Gap Report 2025 discusses these barriers among internet-aware respondents. Those results are not an agricultural literacy census. No verified count of illiterate farmers has been established for this script.

Say **“farmers who struggle with reading or navigating digital tools”** rather than “all farmers are illiterate” or “they do not know the world has advanced.” A person can be highly knowledgeable about farming while finding text-heavy digital services inaccessible. Do not multiply the farm-holding count by a national illiteracy rate to invent an affected-population estimate.

Source: [GSMA Mobile Gender Gap Report 2025](https://www.gsma.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Mobile-Gender-Gap-Report-2025.pdf).

### What the product evidence establishes

The app has 47 passing software tests in the observed session. Database migrations and synthetic encrypted-profile persistence/access checks passed. These support specific software claims. They do not establish reduced crop losses, better yields, household savings, national reach, perfect dialect comprehension, guaranteed safe spoken guidance or a completed farmer pilot.

## Recording and edit rules

1. Record the actual phone demonstration first. A scripted outcome is a recording target, not evidence that it has occurred.
2. Use fictional profile information and a designated synthetic case for the free Gemini Developer API. The free API's content-use terms differ from Vertex's; real farmer information must not be casually introduced into the demo.
3. Keep the main 180-second windows. Rehearse the narration with natural pauses. If the AI takes longer, select an honestly labelled recorded excerpt rather than making a false instant-service claim.
4. For a successful complete registration montage, preserve an unedited source take and the saved/reloaded case. If that is unavailable, retain the current-evidence wording and backend-test labels.
5. Do not stage a competitor hotline failure, call an unrelated service to occupy it, or claim the product is faster without a fair measured comparison.
6. Use close, clean speech recording. Duck or remove music under the phone. Subtitle Bangla precisely. The audience must hear the experience that the pitch is asking them to trust.
7. Avoid empty-plate imagery, fabricated debt figures, invented testimonials, and a helpless portrayal of the farmer. Let the consequences and the access barrier create the emotion.
8. Hide credentials, sign-in links, real phone numbers, raw caller identities and private profile records. Inspect the exported video for leaks.
9. Keep the closing farmer shot and product card inside three minutes. Export and watch the final file at ordinary playback speed before submission.

## Rubric coverage

The supplied AIBC Participants Guide Section 9.2 establishes the five time windows. Section 11.1 gives 20 points each to innovation/AI-native design, technical architecture, commercial viability, scale/impact, and responsible AI/data sovereignty/presentation.

- **Innovation:** a telephone-based Bangla intake and confirmation workflow, demonstrated through the call.
- **Technical architecture:** live speech, backend state control, encrypted records and governed keyword/vector retrieval.
- **Commercial viability:** prospective institutional sponsors and cost per completed case; no fictional revenue.
- **Scale and impact:** a large farming sector, an initial rice focus, measurable supervised validation and locally adapted expansion.
- **Responsible AI and presentation:** consent, correction, scope, uncertainty, provenance and explicit handling of prototype limitations.

This is a script and director's guide. No film, complete phone-registration recording, field study or agronomic outcome was produced by writing it.
