# Agritech voice access video pitch

Prepared 19 November 2026. A production concept and script for the AIBC team competition preliminary submission. This is not a rendered film. The three-minute structure follows sections 9.2 and 11.1 of the supplied Participants Guide.

## The film we should make

**Creative title: A season of work. One unanswered question.**

Follow one fictional rice farmer from an uncertain observation to a clearer question and a structured support case, using an ordinary telephone. Let the audience hear the farmer before they see the software. Reveal the live operator workspace when it explains how the service listens, confirms and builds context. End with a credible vision of a personal agricultural helper accessible by voice.

The problem is usable access to relevant knowledge. A person may own a smartphone and still find searching, evaluating sources, interpreting measurements or navigating an app difficult. Literacy, digital skills, language, affordability and connectivity are distinct barriers. Do not collapse them into an unsupported assertion that most farmers are illiterate. Show a farmer with experience and judgment who needs access to information beyond their immediate reach.

**Product promise:** Agricultural support that starts with a question you can speak, through the phone you already know how to use.

**Recall:** Return to the same question and the same phone at the end. The farmer has a next step and a support pathway; the film does not need to claim a saved harvest.

## The opening

Black screen. Hear a real field recording of wind, then a button press. No music for the first sentence. A farmer says:

> ভাই, ধানের পাতাগুলো হলুদ হয়ে যাচ্ছে। এখন আমি কী করব?

Subtitle: “The rice leaves are turning yellow. What should I do now?”

Cut to a hand holding an ordinary phone beside a rice plant. Identify an acted scene as “Illustrative scenario.” Do not show someone spraying an unidentified pesticide or diagnosing the plant by appearance.

Headline:

> A season of work. One unanswered question.

Narration connects the question to the cost of uncertainty. Keep the farmer's dignity central: the barrier is accessing and applying information, not a lack of intelligence.

An alternative opening for a presenter-led film is: “When a farmer asks, ‘What do I do now?’, how much technology should they have to understand before someone answers?” Use this only if a convincing farmer scene cannot be filmed; do not stack multiple hooks.

## Three-minute script and storyboard

Timing is an edit target, not a guarantee. Rehearse the English narration and leave space for Bangla audio and subtitles. The 60-second demo section uses actual recordings or an explicitly labelled walkthrough, never synthetic footage presented as a successful live call.

### 00:00–00:30 Problem

**Visual:** Farmer, leaf, ordinary phone. Brief inserts of an unfamiliar search page and an app interface, acted rather than presented as research evidence. Product branding has not appeared yet.

**Farmer, approximately five seconds:**

“ভাই, ধানের পাতাগুলো হলুদ হয়ে যাচ্ছে। এখন আমি কী করব?”

**Narrator:**

“For a farmer, that question can decide what happens to a season's work. Useful information may already exist online. Reaching it can require reading unfamiliar text, searching for the right terms, or navigating a smartphone. Owning a phone does not automatically make that knowledge accessible.”

**On-screen words:** “Access. Understanding. A next step.” Show these sequentially, not as a dense slide.

### 00:30–01:00 Solution

**Visual:** Keep the same phone in frame. Introduce Agritech. Reveal a simple line from the phone to a voice orb; the farmer does not open an app. The operator UI is a separate view.

**Narrator:**

“Agritech brings agricultural support into an ordinary phone conversation. A farmer speaks in their own words. The assistant asks permission, confirms important details, and narrows the problem one question at a time. Our goal is a personal agricultural helper that remembers verified context and makes useful information understandable over a call.”

**Small factual caption:** “Caller needs voice-network coverage. The service backend needs internet access.”

**On-screen headline:** “Speak the question. Start the support.”

### 01:00–02:00 Demo or flow

**01:00–01:06, narrator:**

“Here is the prototype. The farmer's interface is the call; this screen is the operator's view.”

**01:06–01:21, real registration excerpts:**

Show the physical phone and the current workspace. Play actual greeting and consent audio, then a short registration excerpt. Use fictional test details and mask the test number.

Target beats, not a fabricated transcript:

| Speaker | Intended Bangla beat | English subtitle |
|---|---|---|
| Assistant | আসসালামু আলাইকুম। আমি কৃষি সহায়তার একটি এআই সহকারী। | Assalamu alaikum. I am an AI agricultural support assistant. |
| Assistant | আপনার তথ্য এই সেবার জন্য সংরক্ষণ করতে পারি? | May we store your details for this service? |
| Caller | জি, রাখতে পারেন। | Yes, you may. |

The full test must include the actual consent read-back and confirmation. Excerpts can shorten the film, but label edited pauses and do not imply a shorter end-to-end registration time.

**01:21–01:35, correction proof:**

Show a scripted correction using fictional information. The caller changes “মহেশপুর” to “মহাদেবপুর.” Let the agent read back the corrected location. On the actual UI, show a proposal before it becomes confirmed. Present a successful save only if the corresponding phone session genuinely completed it.

**Narrator:**

“Corrections remain proposals until the caller confirms them.”

**01:35–01:48, problem narrowing:**

Caller: “ধানের পাতা হলুদ হয়ে গেছে।”

Assistant target: “পুরো পাতা হলুদ, নাকি শুধু ডগা বা কিনারা?”

Subtitles: “The rice leaves have turned yellow.” / “Is the whole leaf yellow, or only the tips or edges?”

The leaf colour is an observation, not a diagnosis. Do not invent a product recommendation, dose or successful agricultural outcome.

**01:48–02:00, narrator over evidence view:**

“The next step depends on the case and the evidence available. Missing or unclear information calls for another question or specialist support.”

**Required recording labels:** “Research prototype · fictional test data.” If applicable, “Pauses shortened.” No fabricated source cards or made-up reasoning.

**If phone registration still does not complete:** Replace its success footage with the label “Registration workflow under phone validation.” Show the genuine call separately from a labelled backend test or workflow walkthrough. Say: “Voice calling is working; this registration workflow is still being validated end to end.” The supplied preliminary guide permits an early prototype, mock flow or walkthrough. Do not splice separate successful components into an apparent single successful call.

### 02:00–02:30 AI approach

**Visual:** A five-block architecture, revealed gradually:

Ordinary phone → live Bangla voice → confirmation tools → encrypted case → reviewed retrieval.

Show noise, uncertainty and confirmation checks as gates, not evidence of perfect accuracy. Display only backend activity that actually occurred.

**Narrator:**

“Gemini Live handles the voice conversation. Backend tools manage confirmed facts, encrypted profiles and retrieval eligibility. Our retrieval framework is built; specialist-reviewed rice content is still being prepared. We are testing safeguards for unclear speech, background noise and uncertain confirmation, so a fluent answer is never treated as evidence of a saved fact.”

**Small footer:** “Prototype validation continues · farmer records encrypted · cloud processing disclosed.” Do not claim local-only processing or Bangladesh-only data residency. Encryption does not establish data sovereignty by itself.

### 02:30–03:00 Impact and next step

**Visual:** A future-service panel with three clearly marked “Planned” cards: weather context, crop market information and seasonal crop planning. Show a cooperative or NGO as a prospective sponsor without using unapproved partner logos. Return to the opening phone.

**Narrator:**

“Next, we plan to connect weather, crop-market information and local growing guidance. Cooperatives, NGOs or extension programmes could sponsor access. A supervised pilot will measure useful-case completion, farmer understanding, response time and cost. We start with rice; the wider vision is personal support through voice. A season of work deserves an answer the farmer can use.”

**End card:**

Agritech

“Knowledge, within calling distance.”

Real team names and roles. Repository or project QR only if the destination is suitable for public viewing and contains no credentials.

## The visual centrepiece

Use the actual green/red/yellow operator workspace. The orb responds to real input and playback audio. Transcript details can appear beside profile cards, but distinguish provisional captions, proposals, corrections and confirmed facts. Source cards appear only after a real retrieval result.

Keep the farmer's phone visible in picture-in-picture for part of the demo. This prevents judges mistaking the operator dashboard for an app the farmer must use. Capture a physical feature phone if claiming feature-phone access; a smartphone demonstration alone does not prove it.

A real elapsed-time counter can strengthen the demonstration. Keep it accurate. If the system stalls, use the honest walkthrough cut rather than disguising the delay or manufacturing an instant response. Do not stage a slow competing helpline and imply it represents real measured performance.

Sound design matters more than elaborate effects. Let the first farmer question play in near silence. Build a restrained music bed during the solution. Duck it completely for the important phone exchange. Bring it back for the final vision. Make the voice and English subtitles intelligible on an ordinary laptop speaker.

## Differentiators to make visible

| Differentiator | What it means for the caller | Proof in this film |
|---|---|---|
| Telephone access | No app navigation or mobile data interface required of the farmer | Physical ordinary-phone call |
| Conversation that narrows the problem | The caller can start with an observation instead of knowing technical vocabulary | One useful follow-up about the symptom |
| Confirmation and correction | The farmer can hear and correct important details | Actual proposed-to-confirmed state and a correction |
| Context with consent | Later support can use verified details instead of treating every exchange as unrelated | Real case fields; explain shared-phone safeguards |
| Evidence boundaries | The service can identify missing information instead of improvising a prescription | Actual evidence availability and a safe fallback |
| Institutional access model | A sponsor could make the service practical for a farmer to use | Proposed payer and measurable pilot plan |

These are design and implementation differentiators, not proof that no competitor offers similar capabilities.

## The wider vision

Keep the three-minute film focused on agricultural support. The broader ambition is a voice access channel for people who struggle with app-based services. Over time, the same intake and context pattern could support public-service navigation, disaster-preparedness information and preliminary intake before a human specialist joins.

Emergency response is a separate, high-risk expansion. Do not claim that the current prototype replaces emergency services, reliably triages emergencies or removes the need for trained humans. Such uses would require verified institutional routing, dependable availability and domain-specific validation. The credible promise is to begin collecting and clarifying information at first contact, then connect it to the appropriate support pathway.

Weather, prices and crop planning need current sources, location and date context, local units, licensing decisions and domain review. A market quote is not a guaranteed selling price. The system must not infer a device type, literacy level or precise location from a phone number alone.

## Rubric alignment

| Official component, 20 points each | Where the film supplies the argument |
|---|---|
| Innovation and AI Native Design | Spoken access through an existing phone, with adaptive intake and context rather than a conventional app interface |
| Technical Excellence and Architecture | Live voice, confirmation gates, encrypted records, retrieval flow and clear limits |
| Business Value and Commercial Viability | A prospective institutional payer and a pilot that measures useful completion and cost |
| Global Scalability and Real World Impact | A focused Bangladesh rice pilot with localization required before expanding to other regions or services |
| Responsible AI Data Sovereignty and Presentation | Consent, correction, shared-phone identity boundaries, cloud-processing disclosure, evidence provenance and honest demo labels |

Do not promise an exact score. A strong film cannot substitute for working evidence, reviewed content, user research or a viable operating model.

## Production checklist

1. Record the actual phone test before locking narration about what works. Use fictional details, consented recordings and no visible credentials.
2. Assign a farmer actor, narrator or presenter, operator, and camera/editor. Team members can cover multiple roles. Do not invent external endorsements.
3. Capture the physical phone, field or plant inserts, operator screen, real correction and real retrieval status.
4. Record English narration after selecting the successful-demo or honest-walkthrough cut. Subtitle the Bangla that was actually spoken.
5. Label acted scenarios and planned features. Remove unsupported claims about majority illiteracy, zero waiting, perfect dialect handling, fully offline service, free calls, approved treatments or established partnerships.
6. Edit to the guide's exact 180-second structure. Time the spoken recording rather than relying on word count. Watch the final film with sound on and once with subtitles only.
7. Keep the final image on the same ordinary phone introduced at the opening. Let the last line complete the farmer's question rather than ending on a list of technologies.

Not produced with this document: a rendered video, a validated feature-phone recording, measured agricultural outcomes or a complete successful phone-registration recording. These remain evidence to collect before the final edit.
